03 / Behind the dashboard

Data Processing

  1. Select the dataset

    The selected year determines which dataset is used.

  2. Use prepared scores

    The platform reads wellbeing scores already stored in the dataset.

  3. Apply filters

    Year, gender, grade and SES selections shape the requested view.

  4. Display results

    The dashboard presents charts, percentages and summaries.

More about data processing

Questionnaire scores are prepared before dashboard analysis. Missing scores are excluded for that indicator; missing results do not mean zero wellbeing. Some response-category measures have additional eligibility rules.

Low, Mid and High SES selections exclude unknown or invalid deciles. At least one band stays selected; there is no separate All button. Postcode mappings need checking for each year.

Understanding the results

Higher scores do not always mean better wellbeing. Use each indicator's scoring guidance. Symptom categories are not clinical diagnoses.

Scoring guidance and other indicators

Exact ranges and thresholds require the approved RYA Scoring Instructions. Anxiety's score direction and category mapping remain unverified; numerical cutoffs are not reproduced here. Scores should not be compared across different indicators.

Other indicators cover School Engagement, Friendship, Family, Bullying, Healthy Eating, Adequate Sleep, Physically Active and Social Media. Available views vary; previews are not survey findings.

05 / The numbers explained

Understanding the Results

Percentages
The share of included students in each category.
Average scores
The average wellbeing score for the selected group.
Group comparisons
Results shown across available student groups.
Statistical testing
Some Depression comparisons assess evidence for a difference.
Technical details

Depression comparisons use a two-sided Mann–Whitney U test on score ranks (normal approximation, corrected for ties) and a two-proportion z-test on the share with high symptoms, with p < 0.05 as the threshold.

These tests do not establish causation or practical importance. The rank test does not directly test averages. Small groups need caution, and multiple comparisons are not adjusted, increasing the chance of chance findings. A non-significant result does not prove equality.

Averages can hide variation. Percentages depend on eligible responses and rounding, so they may not total 100%. Do not assume other indicators use these tests.

06 / Four reading tips

Data Interpretation

  1. Check your filters

    Year, gender, grade and SES selections affect the requested view.

  2. Understand score direction

    Higher scores do not always mean better wellbeing.

  3. Compare carefully

    Group differences show patterns, not causes. Survey years may include different students or measures.

  4. Read the context

    Check labels, group definitions and sample sizes. Comparison summaries may cover different groups from the filtered total.

07 / Responsible use

Limitations & References

Limitations

  • Results are based on student-reported responses.
  • Participants may not represent all Australian students.
  • Response numbers can differ between indicators.
  • Area-based SES does not describe individual circumstances.

Key Sources

  • RYA Scoring Instructions

Protect small groups

Groups with fewer than 10 students require privacy protection. Review results against approved reporting rules before publication; automatic suppression is not confirmed.
